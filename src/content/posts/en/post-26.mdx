# 1. Why I'm Interested in Agents

The most direct reason for my sustained interest in Agents stems from the needs of indie development. In traditional development, one person's time and energy are ultimately limited, and there is an upper bound on output and coverage. But with Agent collaboration, not only can the effectiveness and quality of existing work be significantly enhanced, but a team of intelligent agents can also break through the capability and resource constraints of the development pipeline, independently supporting the entire product lifecycle. This leverage effect holds enormous appeal for indie developers whose time resources are precious.

Another point of interest lies in the massive potential embedded in the evolution of computing architecture paradigms and human-machine collaboration paradigms brought about by Agents. The computer industry has achieved enormous productivity leaps over the past few decades, all based on CPUs and the von Neumann architecture, characterized by **computational power** as the core tool form providing services, with humans acting as **peripheral devices** in human-machine collaboration, supplying intelligence and environmental interaction. Now, under the Agent paradigm, computers provide services centered on **intelligence** while also offering richer environmental perception and operation. Rather than existing as tools, they more closely resemble intelligent living beings. In the human-machine interaction paradigm, humans no longer need to function as peripheral devices — they can interact naturally and collaborate complementarily with agent-like entities, co-evolving together. Intelligent agents may have replaced part of the human workload in previous models, but viewed from another angle, the value of humans as tools is diminishing — it is human existence and uniqueness that represent true value. All of this is already happening, and to understand and adapt to it, learning and using the Agent paradigm is a good start.

# 2. Why Use Hermes Bots

Several months ago, I wanted to use OpenClaw for some things. At the time, all the major media outlets were promoting it as omnipotent, but even my most basic daily news wasn't stable enough. After switching models there was some improvement, but stability remained low. Plus, every version upgrade essentially required lengthy manual fixes. Eventually, I felt the costs outweighed the benefits and gradually gave up. After shelving it for a few months and reading some reviews, I found that the earlier hype had been overblown — in real-world use, everyone actually had the same experience, and it wasn't just me being incompetent.

Hermes certainly didn't have the same level of hype as OpenClaw at the time, and from its homepage it felt quite personalized and a bit niche. However, several months have proven that it is indeed superior:

- First is the stability of features and versions, which is actually very important.
- Second is a better self-learning system. I think the difference may come from design philosophy — products like Claude Code seem to be designed as coding tools, so much of the memory and storage require user-initiated actions. Hermes, by contrast, is designed more like a living organism that learns and grows on its own. Claude Code is excellent as a coding assistant, but for a virtual team, Hermes' mechanism better fits the scenario.
- Finally, it saves tokens. OpenClaw is quite token-hungry; thanks to its prompt caching mechanism, Hermes is much better in this regard.

Recently, Hermes also added the Bots mode, making each Agent's context more streamlined, and Skill design can be more specialized. In terms of efficiency, accuracy, and cost, using Hermes Bots is better suited for building Agent teams.

# 3. Why Use a Middle-earth-themed Agent Team

Tolkien's *The Lord of the Rings* constructed a grand, epic, complex, and complete world. There is a tradition in hacker history of using Middle-earth place names — as early as the late 1960s, hackers at Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (SAIL) in California named their server room "Mordor" and the break room "The Prancing Pony"; later, the Silicon Valley company Palantir did the same. The distinctive character personalities are now also very well-suited as personas and team metaphors for Agents.

# 4. My Agent Team Design

The Hermes desktop app makes it easy to create Bots. I selected several characters from *The Lord of the Rings*, gave them their own personalities, abilities, and responsibilities, and organized them into different groups.

## 🍀 The Hidden Character

The hidden character is Frodo, the protagonist of *The Lord of the Rings*. Compared to the many characters with divine powers and great might, he appears small and ordinary, even somewhat fragile. But he possesses a resilience and pure heart that no one else has. Moreover, he is the one who connects the peaceful life of the Shire to the long journey of the Ring. Unlike the other unreliable team members, he truly completed the entire journey — just as we exist in the world and must traverse our own life journey. He is not designed as a Bot; he is a metaphor for ourselves.

# 5. Configuration TIPS

- **Performance Optimization**
  - **Streamline skills & tools**: After creating multiple Bots, you will noticeably experience lag and loading delays when switching. Streamlining each bot's skills and tools can dramatically improve loading performance.
  - **Multiplexed gateway**: Refer to
    [multi-profile-gateways](https://hermes-agent.ac.cn/docs/user-guide/multi-profile-gateways)
    — just add one configuration, and multiple bots can share a single gateway,
    reducing operating and maintenance costs.

- **Feishu (Lark) Channel Configuration**
  - **Bot 1:1 Feishu app configuration**:
    Compared to configuring just 1 Feishu app to forward content to internal bots,
    setting up multiple bots is more hassle to configure but offers better message
    processing speed, independence, and ease of use.
    <img src="/images/posts/26/5.webp" class="w-2/3" />
  - **Chat groups**:
    Feishu supports adding multiple bots to a single group; you can invoke them
    via @ mentions to send corresponding content. The difference from the Hermes
    desktop app is that bots cannot receive broadcast messages in the group,
    but this is acceptable from a performance and overhead perspective.

# 6. Follow-up Work

## Continuous Tuning

Unlike the traditional notion of software releases delivering complete functionality, Agent configuration is not an end but a beginning. Just like day-to-day living, continuous iteration is the source of intelligence and capability. It requires extensive data tuning and ongoing conversations and validation integrated with life and work to unleash its full power.

Continuously refining better souls and skills for each Agent is a bit like a D&D game — you can mix and match different characters, each with different skills, ultimately building a better combination to complete adventure quests.

Using Agents as a medium to record knowledge is also more useful than pure text notes.

## Caring About Life and Work

An Agent team is not made of real people — it is actually a projection of one's own thinking and capabilities. Only by better understanding one's own work and life, and possessing a clearer worldview, outlook on life, and values, can one endow them with more effective capabilities and souls.
